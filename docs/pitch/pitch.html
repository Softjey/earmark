The problem

In 2017, Poles donated to save a boy's sight. The boy never existed.

6 500

people donated on a Polish crowdfunding platform

500k zł

raised for "Antoś", a made-up story

1

organizer received it all and spent it on himself

The platform held the money and paid the organizer. Everything else was trust. More vetting by the platform only moves that trust from one party to another.

Earmark02
The solution

A vault with exactly two exits, written in code

A database operator can edit balances and pay anyone. On Solana, these two exits are code that even its authors cannot bypass, and anyone can check every transfer.

Earmark03
How it works

Four steps, each one a public transaction

1

Verify once

A verifier links a wallet to a registered organisation (KRS, RPWDL). Once per organisation, not per fundraiser.

2

Create

The organizer sets the recipient, target and deadline, plus the SHA-256 of the invoice. These cannot be changed later.

3

Confirm

The recipient confirms the document hash on-chain. No donations are accepted before that.

4

Pay out or refund

The donation that hits the target pays the recipient in the same transaction. Otherwise a keeper refunds every donor.

Earmark05
Remaining trust, and its limits

One question left: does this wallet belong to this organisation?

Bounded

The verifier can't move money

The worst case if its key leaks is a fake recipient. That is visible on-chain, and recipients verified in the last 7 days are flagged on /audit.

Re-checkable

Check it yourself

The registry number is stored on-chain, and every fundraiser page links to the official KRS / RPWDL search. Any invoice file can be checked against the confirmed hash right in the browser.

Production

Not us

A multisig council of independent parties, then attestations from banks' KYB checks via the Solana Attestation Service.

If the organizer and the recipient collude, the thief is a registered legal entity that signed the invoice on-chain under its own name, not an anonymous person.
